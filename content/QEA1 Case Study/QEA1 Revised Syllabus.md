# Course Syllabus

## What is Quantitative Engineering Analysis?

Using quantitative analysis of models and data to make choices is critical for success in the engineering design process.  Whether you are choosing an appropriate material, sizing a component, determining the effect of certain influences on your design, or optimizing your design within a parameter space, you often need to obtain (through experiment or calculation), interpret quantitative information to inform your decisions, and communicate those decisions and reasoning. There are many different approaches to getting and interpreting the data you need to make your decision: you may wish to conduct an experiment, do a rough estimation, perform a detailed calculation based on mathematical models of your system, or create a computer simulation. If you want to engineer effectively, you must be able to choose and use appropriate quantitative approaches for a given situation. This course is part of a sequence of courses to help you develop these skills.

In QEA 1, we'll be learning about quantitative engineering analysis in the context of facial recognition. However, the class is about more than learning math and engineering content.

## Learning Philosophy

We have intentionally designed the QEA experience with the goal of success for every student. To accomplish this, we need you as active participants in designing this class and making it the best it can be. Learning and discussions work best when everyone is engaged.  We want to emphasize four philosophical points:

1. Your physical and mental health is very important to us. Please reach out if you ever feel overwhelmed or want to chat. 

2. Strive to be an active learner and risk taker in this course. This means not only participating in class and doing homework, but actively engaging in the material to make connections between concrete examples and the more general mathematical principles under investigation. After you've finished each problem or computational project, step back to reflect on the core ideas you've learned from the exercise.

3. Ask questions and provide feedback! Visit faculty office hours for either of these types of communication, or just to chat about hobbies, travels, etc. We will be asking you for regular feedback on how things are going, and we also invite you to let us know at any time if you think there are things we should be aware of. We can’t guarantee that all feedback will result in change, but we pledge to listen to and consider feedback with an open mind.

4. While you will be encouraged to collaborate with your peers on almost all components of this course, the end goal of every conversation, exercise, and project is that everyone on the team / at the table has contributed to and understands each part of the work. This is in contrast to some courses at Olin where team projects are conducted with more of a divide-and-conquer approach where each team member has a distinct role. In QEA, every team member has the same role, which is a joint learning and teaching role.

## Course Structure

### The Big Picture

This class is intended to help you understand how to undertake a quantitative analysis of an engineering problem, so that when you are faced with a complicated engineering challenge, you can break it up into well-defined questions and understand which tools will be useful in helping you to solve this problem, and be able to communicate about your use of these tools and your reasoning for using them. This class is about quantitative design, and being able to understand what is 'under the hood' of computational tools that you will be using in your everyday engineering world. This course is designed around one main challenge (facial recognition), but will be broken up into modules to help you learn the key building blocks and understand how these concepts can be applied to other engineering problems. 

### In-Class Work

Each day there will be an in-class document that provides a set of readings and activities for the class period. Generally speaking, you will work through this document collaboratively with your team, with faculty and course assistants dropping in to provide support. In-class activities may include, but are certainly not limited to, debriefs of the previous homework assignment, micro-lectures, exercises, and project work.

### Homework Assignments

There will be homework assignments that dive deeper into key conceptual material that was introduced in class. We will often provide some of the solutions along with the homework assignments in order to better facilitate self-directed learning. There will be two deliverables associated with each homework assignment.

- Deliverable 1: a Canvas survey that asks you to evaluate your engagement on the assignment, reflect on things that went well for you, identify areas for improvement, and list particular concepts that you are still struggling with.  You will also submit your work for the assignment, but its correctness will not factor into your grade.  We will look at these from time-to-time to get a better sense of how folks are doing with the material (but it will not be used to evaluate you as an individual). The weekly homework and corresponding survey will typically be due at 10:59PM on Monday evenings, but some assignments may be due on Thursday evenings.
    
- Deliverable 2: a diagnostic quiz on Canvas associated with each assignment and tied to specific learning objectives for the course.  This will allow you to test whether you've understood some of the key concepts.  That said, the quizzes do not test every concept that is important on the assignment, so it is important to work through all of the problems on the assignment (and not focus only on the quiz material).  You should complete the diagnostic quiz individually (even if you are working through the assignment with someone else).  This is important to help you build confidence that you are mastering the material. The weekly diagnostic quiz will typically be due at 10:59PM on Tuesday or Friday evenings. If you feel good about the homework, you can certainly do the diagnostic quiz earlier in the week; however, by having them due a day later than the homework, you also have a chance to ask more questions during our class debriefs. You can retake the quizzes, but there will be a time buffer before you can take it again. This is to help you pause and reflect on the material and revisit it later (spaced repetition is helpful for many types of learning). 
    
- Deliverable 3: An oral assessment associated with each module to assess your understanding of the topics, as well as your rhetorical ability to describe various parts of the module. As we progress through the semester we will draw back on these from time-to-time as a secondary gauge of the students' understanding, as well as their rhetorical abilities. Spreadsheets will be posted in announcements a week before the start of each new module in which you can sign up for the various timeslots for your oral assessments. This is graded on completion and is focused on the development of your rhetorical skills and technical understanding. 
    

We encourage a workflow similar to

1. Read the whole assignment at a surface level. Identify material you may have seen before, and concepts that are new to you. Note sections where you might want to seek additional resources, either written or in person.
    
2. Attempt the exercises without the solutions (work together or independently). If you are really struggling, move on, often the best way to approach an assignment is not linearly. Mark exercises you don’t understand so you can come back to them.
    
3. Go to office hours to get help and co-work. 
    
4. Finish the assignment to the best of your ability.
    
5. Submit the homework survey on Canvas to reflect on your approach to the assignment.
    
6. When the solutions are posted, review your homework with the solutions and identify areas of confusion.
    
7. Take the diagnostic quiz (this is an individual activity). 
    
8. If you did not answer all questions correctly on the diagnostic quiz, identify the learning outcomes that need more attention and then retake the learning goal specific quiz.
    

### Project

For each QEA module, you will be working with one or more colleagues to solve engineering challenges that require a quantitative solution.  In some modules this will take the form of one large challenge, and in other modules you may solve a series of challenges. For this fall, we'll just have one project at the end of the semester, with a deliverable of a poster presentation. The projects give you a chance to synthesize the knowledge you've learned from the module, put your own stamp on the work by pushing the project in interesting directions as you see fit, and hopefully have a lot of fun in the process by creating something you are truly proud of.

### Table Assignments

Generally for class, you will be randomly assigned to a room and table team within that room.  We randomize team assignments so students have the opportunity to work with a variety of classmates and different faculty throughout the semester.  At some point in the semester, we may give you some choice around teaming, but we ask you to start with openness and flexibility. 

### Collaboration

A lot of people learn best by working with others, and we encourage you to do this if it works for you. That means that it's fine to talk to a bunch of people about how to approach an exercise, but you should be able to confidently explain the concepts, and you should take the conceptual quiz individually. On projects, you'll be working with other folks, but each person should be actively involved with every aspect of the project (rather than, for example, one person doing coding and another doing the write-up).  We ask you to work together so you will learn from each other and learn to collaborate.  We (the teaching team) are also excited to work with you all, so please come to our office hours (where we will be waiting patiently, hoping to see your lovely faces so we can work with you to help you better understand the material!).

## Constructive Engagement

QEA is designed around the philosophy that consistent engagement with the material will lead to learning and your ultimate success in the courses (e.g., feeling positive about the material, having productive interactions with your peers).  Moreover, figuring out how to engage with the material in a way that works best for you as a learner is vital.  In this way, it is not simply engagement in terms of time spent with the material, but engaging constructively with the material that will lead to the best outcomes.  We have designed the course to give you the opportunity to figure out what learning strategies work best for you and reward you for working to continuously improve your approach to the course.

When you submit your surveys for the homework assignments we will ask you to evaluate and reflect on your engagement. This is for your benefit and ours. Patterns of poor or good engagement are easy to spot, and don't be surprised if we reach out to you with questions about how you are doing.

## Grading

This course is Pass / No Record. Our intention of this grading system is to prioritize learning over performing. We have designed the deliverables for this class to support learning aligned with the intended learning outcomes of the course. We expect you to meaningfully engage with the deliverables in this course.

This year, we are attempting to use specific deliverables to help us (you and the instructors) estimate your progress on the course learning outcomes. Several Olin students spent time this summer designing how this will work. However, this isn't going to be a perfect system and we need your help to make it better. Please chat with us about what's working and how we might make it better (noting that we are a bit constrained by the capabilities of Canvas at this point). 

- For the learning outcomes of Lifelong Learning, the deliverables and progress on learning objectives will mostly be completion-based. This is because we want you to think and reflect on these concepts, and we don't want you to feel pressure to say particular things to get a good grade. We are here to help you learn and grow. 
    

- For example, we have an objective that includes "Practice seeking and selecting appropriate resources (e.g., books, artificial intelligence, internet, peers, mentors, computational tools, etc)".  Here, we have deliverables (such as homework surveys or class surveys) that ask you to reflect on how you have sought and used these resources. This reflection on your learning process is core to the goal of the class. We don't expect perfection, but we do genuinely desire honesty.  To demonstrate this, an example of an excellent answer to a question about how you used resources for an assignment might be: "I watched a bunch of videos to try to learn about matrix multiplication while folding my laundry. They seemed to make sense while I was watching them, but then I still couldn't do it. I think I need to actively take notes while I watch. But at least I folded my laundry! I mostly worked alone, but I think I will try to work with some other students next time to help me start earlier." This wasn't an amazing use of resources, but it was an honest and thoughtful reflection on use of resources. Yay!
    

- For the Discourse and Ethics outcomes, we will assess your learnings based on in class discussions, presentations, and the parts of projects/assignments that are related to synthesis, explanation, and reasoning. 
    

- For example, during an in class discussion about the ethics of AI, the ability to concisely and clearly state why you think AI is ethical in a certain application will be seen as mastery over learning objectives related to the ethics of AI. Also, if the teaching team sees that you are open to changing your perspective, and enable open discourse, this will be seen as mastery of the discourse learning goals. 
    

- For the Linear Algebra and Coding and Computer Programming learning outcomes, we will provide some diagnostic quizzes (usually paced with the homework) to help you connect questions to learning outcomes and to gauge your progress on each learning outcome. These should show up in the Learning Mastery Gradebook, highlighting your progress on each. We will also assess mastery by your fundamental understanding of base concepts. If you can explain a complex subject in a simple and correct way, you have a mastery of that subject. 
    

- For example, on a diagnostic quiz if there is a question similar to “Explain to your 14 year old brother how his phone can recognize his face” and you are able to explain facial recognition in terms he would understand, that is considered mastery. Also, for the final project presentation, if you can very clearly explain a complex concept in a way that gives other students a good understanding, that is mastery. 
    

If it seems like what is showing up in the Learning Mastery Gradebook doesn't reflect your progress or understanding, don't worry but please do let us know so we can work together to make this new system work better. We are learners too!

We expect you to engage with all of the learning outcomes for the course and to seriously attempt all of the assignments, quizzes, and surveys. If you engage with all or almost all of the course work, you will pass the course. If you start to fall behind on assignments, we should check in.

We expect that you will make progress on the learning objectives. Because we want you to focus on your own growth and your growth mindset, we're not setting a specific threshold around these learning objectives this year. At some point in the semester, we will check in about progress and expectations. For now, just focus on learning.

### Late Policy

Each assignment on Canvas has a due date.  We list these dates to best serve your learning experience in this course and ask you to make every effort to complete the work by the Canvas due date. However, a significant part of this first semester experience is learning how to balance work at Olin, and you will inevitably turn in work late at some point. We will reduce your score on any deliverable by 1% per day late: this late policy is designed to reduce the stress on you for turning in work late, while making it clear that being late is not in your best interest from a learning perspective. As a result of these policies, there is no need for you to ask for extensions on any of the regular course assignments (project assignments are an exception).

We know there are many reasons why you might fall behind in QEA (e.g., an unexpected illness or a flare-up of a chronic condition, difficulty balancing various personal or academic commitments). When asking us for help crafting an individualized plan, please do not feel the need to justify to us why you are falling behind; we are happy to work with you on a plan regardless.

Finally, please, please never skip class because you have not finished an assignment. We'd rather have you attend class and then work out a plan to finish the assignment later.
