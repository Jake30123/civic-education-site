**Civic Education in Engineering**

Olin’s Mission: 

“Olin College prepares students to become exemplary engineering innovators who recognize needs, design solutions, and engage in creative enterprises for the good of the world. Olin is dedicated to continual discovery and development of effective learning approaches and environments, and to co-developing educational transformation with collaborators around the globe.”

  

Doing “good” for the world has two key parts, the “doing” and the “good”. First, one needs to understand what “good” is based on the context of the world we live in. Doing “good” is defined differently from person to person, so one needs an idea of what “good” means to them. We call this understanding of the world critical awareness and your personal perception and beliefs around it as civic identity. As for “doing”, you need to understand how to act effectively to accomplish that “good”. We call this informed type of action critical action. A civic identity is formed and built upon by the every life experiences one has, but Olin can support the development of this identity through supporting critical awareness and critical action. A student needs a developed civic identity to effectively “do good”.

  

So how do you develop a student's civic identity? After researching and building out frameworks over the course of the semester, we came to a few key conclusions:

- The educator should not frame any value as correct or incorrect at the risk of alienating students.
    
- The best way to support the development of civic identity is by teaching critical awareness through historical, philosophical, and compassionate perspectives and frameworks, as well as giving opportunities and frameworks for students to exercise their identity through critical action.
    
- As an institution, we are responsible for giving students these basic skills, as well as providing. spaces for them to engage in discourse and opportunities to take critical action.
    
- At the same time, we also cannot reduce the technical education we provide without disservicing our students.
    

Here is what we have done so far to advance civic education at Olin: 

1. We developed what we are calling a competency tree. This essentially a flow chart illuminating the various competencies that builds up critical awareness and critical action action as competencies, and supports the healthy development of civic identity
    
2. To demonstrate that any of this is possible, we wanted to develop a case study of integrating a skill needed for civic identity into a technical class. The goal of this case study is to show it is not only possible, but enhances a student's engineering ability. Our experimental plan is to add rhetoric and discourse into QEA 1. We wrote a draft syllabus, developed a quiz, a final project, and other materials that integrate the teaching and evaluation of discourse and rhetoric.
